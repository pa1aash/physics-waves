Arm 5 - why the sectoral modes n = m sit below the trend
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Session L3 flagged the m=2, n=2 entry of arm 4 as an unexplained
  outlier. It is not an outlier and it is not numerical: EVERY sectoral
  mode behaves this way, and the cause is structural.

  (i) The pattern is systematic across m:
      m |    n=m      n=m+1     n=m+2
      1 |  -15.78%   -40.38%   -27.84%
      2 |   -7.51%   -19.80%   -15.77%
      3 |   -4.00%   -10.45%    -9.51%
      4 |   -2.34%    -6.08%    -6.10%
      5 |   -1.47%    -3.83%    -4.13%
      sectoral less slowed than its n=m+1 neighbour, every m: yes

  (ii) The cause, demonstrated causally. The Coriolis coupling matrix B
       is tridiagonal: it connects degree n only to n +/- 1. A sectoral
       mode sits at the bottom of the degree ladder (n >= m), so it has
       only ONE neighbour to couple through instead of two -- half the
       channels through which its rotational flow can be converted into
       divergence and surface displacement.

       Test: take a NON-sectoral mode and delete its lower neighbour from
       the basis, leaving its latitudinal structure untouched. If the
       explanation is right, its slowing must collapse toward sectoral
       values.

        m   n   full basis   lower partner removed   true sectoral (n=m)
        1   2    -40.38%         -10.90%               -15.78%
        2   3    -19.80%          -6.44%                -7.51%
        3   4    -10.45%          -3.98%                -4.00%
      removing the partner halves the slowing or better: yes

  Physical statement: a sectoral mode has no meridional node and sits at
  the bottom of the spherical-harmonic degree ladder. The rotation of the
  sphere can hand its rotational flow to only one adjacent degree rather
  than two, so a smaller share of its potential-vorticity budget is
  available to be absorbed by vortex stretching, and it is correspondingly
  less slowed by the free surface. This is a genuine physical special
  case, not a numerical artefact.
