check_ripa_divergent_condition
========================================================================
Claim: Ripa's (1983) divergent stability conditions reduce to the
classical nondivergent condition, and the extra divergent condition
is not binding for this project's jet at any rotation rate it runs.

SOURCE STATUS: Ripa (1983) is ABSTRACT-VERIFIED, not read. The two
conditions below are this project's reading of an abstract that
states them in words. See docs/literature/MISSING.md.

Arm 1 - does Ripa's condition reduce to Rayleigh-Kuo as the surface
        becomes rigid?
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Condition (i)  : (dQ/dy)(c0 - ubar) >= 0 everywhere.
                   This is the Fjortoft-strengthened form of the
                   classical criterion: it still requires dQ/dy to
                   change sign if it is to fail, since a monotone
                   dQ/dy admits a c0 outside the range of ubar.
  Condition (ii) : |c0 - ubar| <= sqrt(gH), the long-gravity-wave speed.

  The nondivergent limit is the rigid-lid limit, in which the free
  surface cannot deflect and long gravity waves become infinitely
  fast. Formally: eps = 4 Omega^2 R^2/(gH) -> 0 means gH -> infinity
  at fixed Omega and R, hence sqrt(gH) -> infinity.

    Earth, H = 10 km    sqrt(gH) =      313.1 m/s   eps =    8.8044
    gH x 100            sqrt(gH) =     3131.5 m/s   eps =    0.0880
    gH x 10^4           sqrt(gH) =    31314.8 m/s   eps =    0.0009

  As eps -> 0, sqrt(gH) -> infinity and condition (ii) is satisfied by
  ANY bounded zonal flow. It therefore drops out, leaving condition (i)
  alone -- the classical criterion.

  VERDICT: Ripa's condition REDUCES CLEANLY to the nondivergent one.
  The divergent case does not modify the classical condition; it ADDS
  a second, independent condition that has no nondivergent analogue.

Arm 2 - is the extra divergent condition binding for this project?
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Galewsky jet: u_max = 80.0 m/s, u_min = 0.0 m/s
  long-gravity-wave speed sqrt(gH)   =   313.15 m/s
  best-case c0 = (u_max + u_min)/2   =    40.00 m/s
  max |c0 - ubar|                    =    40.00 m/s
  margin sqrt(gH) / max|c0 - ubar|   =     7.83x
  Froude-like ratio u_max/sqrt(gH)   =   0.2555

  Even the least favourable admissible c0 -- one at the edge of the
  jet's velocity range rather than its midpoint -- only doubles
  max|c0 - ubar| to 80 m/s, still 3.9x below sqrt(gH).
  The conclusion does not depend on how c0 is chosen.

  condition (ii) binding at margin < 2.0x: NO

  Condition (ii) contains sqrt(gH) and ubar and NO rotation rate, so
  sweeping Omega cannot activate it. Lamb's parameter eps rises from
  0.55 to 141 across the sweep -- the system becomes far more divergent
  in the sense that matters for WAVE dispersion (section 6) -- while the
  divergent STABILITY condition is untouched. Those are two different
  senses of 'divergent', and conflating them is the trap this check
  exists to avoid.

Arm 4 - the regime where the extra condition does bite
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Hayashi & Young (1987), abstract, verbatim: they consider 'the
  instabilities of rotating, shallow-water, shear flows on an
  EQUATORIAL beta-plane', and show that 'when the basic state has no
  potential vorticity gradients an unstable wave has zero wave
  energy'. So instability without a PV-gradient sign change is real --
  but it is an equatorial result.

  Why the equator is different, and why this project is not there:
  the mechanism is that with a free surface the energy density is cubic
  in the field variables, so wave energy is not positive definite and a
  negative-energy wave can grow by GIVING energy to the mean flow. That
  requires the flow to be comparable to the gravity-wave speed. At the
  equator f -> 0 and nothing limits ubar; here Fr = 0.255 << 1.

  This project's jet is midlatitude, geostrophically balanced, and
  strongly subcritical. The counterexample does not reach it.
