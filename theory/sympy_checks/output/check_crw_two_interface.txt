check_crw_two_interface
========================================================================
Claim: the section-10 two-interface counter-propagating Rossby wave
model is an exact reduction for piecewise-constant PV, and yields the
opposite-sign requirement and a short-wave cutoff.

Arm 1 - reduction to the published Rayleigh dispersion relation
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  root C = -sqrt(K**2*exp(2*K) - 2*K*exp(2*K) + exp(2*K) - 1)*exp(-K)/(2*K)  ->  matches published form
  root C = sqrt(K**2*exp(2*K) - 2*K*exp(2*K) + exp(2*K) - 1)*exp(-K)/(2*K)  ->  matches published form
  reference: Heifetz, Bishop & Alpert (1999), QJRMS 125, 2835-2853,
  their Eq. (6) for the Rayleigh constant-shear strip.

Arm 2 - instability requires oppositely signed PV jumps
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  discriminant = Delta_1*Delta_2*exp(-4*b*k)/(4*k**2) + Delta_c**2/4
  the first term is a square, so disc < 0 requires Delta_1 Delta_2 < 0:
  the two interfaces must carry oppositely signed PV jumps. This is
  exactly the section-8 sign change, arrived at as a phase-locking
  requirement rather than as an integral constraint.
  same-sign case is a sum of non-negative terms: yes

Arm 3 - published cutoff, optimum and peak growth rate
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  short-wave cutoff  K_c = 1.2785   published 1.28   |diff| = 0.0015
  most unstable      K_m = 0.7968   published 0.8    |diff| = 0.0032
  peak growth / shear    = 0.2012   published 0.2   |diff| = 0.0012
  Physically: waves shorter than K_c cannot reach across the jet
  (the coupling carries exp(-2K)), and waves much longer than K_m
  counter-propagate too fast to hold a fixed phase against the shear.
  Instability lives in the window between those two failures.

Arm 4 - mapping to a spherical zonal wavenumber (readout)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  dQ/dphi sign changes at: 32.20 deg, 39.79 deg, 49.82 deg, 58.16 deg
  reversed-gradient bands centred at 36.00 deg and 53.99 deg
  -> phi_j = 44.99 deg, b_eff = 1001 km
  toy-model m* = K_m cos(phi_j) R / (2 b_eff) = 1.79
  solved eigenvalue problem (section 9) gives m* = 6

  THESE DISAGREE, by a factor of about 3.3, and the disagreement is reported rather
  than tuned away. Two identified reasons, both physical:
   (i) the toy model concentrates each PV gradient into a delta
       function, which maximises the long-range reach of the inverted
       flow and so biases the optimum toward longer waves than a
       smoothly distributed gradient supports;
   (ii) the Galewsky jet has *three* distinct gradient regions, not
        two -- a positive core between the two reversed bands -- so a
        two-interface reduction omits one of the interacting waves
        entirely.
  The toy model's standing claims are therefore the mechanism, the
  opposite-sign requirement, the short-wave cutoff and the scaling
  m* ~ cos(phi_j) R / b_eff -- not the absolute value of m*. Section 9
  supplies that. This arm is a readout, not a pass/fail gate.
