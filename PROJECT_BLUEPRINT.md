## 1. Project identity

**Working title.** Beta-Effect Control of Barotropic Rossby-Wave Dispersion and Zonal-Jet Instability on a Rotating Sphere

**One-line summary.** A fully computational spectral study of how the latitudinal gradient of planetary vorticity governs the westward propagation, meridional structure and shear instability of large-scale barotropic waves in a rotating shallow-water fluid on a sphere.

**Keywords.** Geophysical fluid dynamics, rotating shallow water, beta-effect, Rossby waves, Rossby-Haurwitz waves, barotropic instability, Rayleigh-Kuo criterion, spectral methods, spherical harmonics, potential vorticity, Dedalus, ERA5.

**Abstract (draft, approximately 250 words).**

Large-scale atmospheric motion is dominated by slow, planetary-scale waves that propagate systematically westward relative to the mean flow. Their existence follows from the beta-effect, the latitudinal variation of the Coriolis parameter on a rotating sphere, which supplies a restoring gradient of planetary vorticity. This project quantifies how strongly that gradient controls wave behaviour, using a purely computational approach with no laboratory component. The rotating shallow-water equations are solved on the full sphere by a spectral Galerkin method in a spherical-harmonic basis, with implicit-explicit Runge-Kutta time integration. The governing system is first derived in vector-invariant form and linearised twice, about a state of rest to obtain the barotropic Rossby-wave dispersion relation, and about a zonal jet to obtain the barotropic instability problem and the Rayleigh-Kuo necessary condition. The solver is verified against the standard spherical shallow-water benchmark suite, using closed-form solutions where they exist and a self-generated high-resolution reference where they do not, together with a spectral-convergence study and conservation diagnostics for mass, energy and potential enstrophy. Controlled numerical experiments then sweep planetary rotation rate and zonal wavenumber, measuring westward phase speed from space-time diagrams and comparing it against the analytic Rossby-Haurwitz prediction. Meridional structure and turning latitudes are extracted, and model scales are compared against wave signatures diagnosed from ERA5 reanalysis. Finally, zonal jets of varying meridional shear, including a profile derived from observations, are perturbed to measure exponential growth rates and dominant unstable wavenumbers against linear stability theory. Numerical uncertainty is quantified by resolution convergence and parameter sensitivity.

**Nature of the work.** Purely computational. No experimental or laboratory component. All primary data is generated by numerical simulation; external data is used exclusively for verification and validation.

## 2. Scientific motivation and context

### 2.1 The physical problem

The atmosphere at mid-latitudes is not dominated by small-scale turbulence but by slow, coherent, planetary-scale waves. These are the meanders of the jet stream, with wavelengths of thousands of kilometres, and they organise the sequence of weather systems that pass over a given location. They possess a defining and counterintuitive property. Relative to the background flow they always propagate westward, never eastward.

The reason is the beta-effect. On a rotating sphere the local vertical component of the planetary rotation vector, expressed through the Coriolis parameter f = 2 Omega sin(phi), increases monotonically from zero at the equator to a maximum at the poles. A fluid column displaced meridionally therefore arrives in a region of different planetary vorticity. Because potential vorticity is materially conserved, the column must acquire compensating relative vorticity, and the resulting vorticity pattern induces a velocity field that propagates the disturbance westward. The gradient beta = df/dy is thus a restoring mechanism in exactly the way that gravity is for surface waves.

A second consequence of the same background rotation is that sufficiently sheared zonal flows become unstable. The Rayleigh-Kuo criterion states that a necessary condition for barotropic instability is that the meridional gradient of absolute vorticity changes sign somewhere in the domain. Because beta contributes positively to that gradient everywhere, planetary rotation is stabilising, and instability requires the curvature of the jet to overcome it. The same parameter therefore governs both wave propagation and flow stability, which is what makes it worth isolating.

### 2.2 What is established

The linear theory is classical and secure. The dispersion relation for barotropic Rossby waves is standard textbook material, as is the Rossby-Haurwitz solution for a single spherical-harmonic mode on the sphere and the Rayleigh-Kuo necessary condition. The numerical side is equally mature. A standard benchmark suite for the spherical shallow-water equations has been the accepted test for numerical schemes in this domain for three decades, and high-accuracy spectral reference solutions for it are documented in the literature.

### 2.3 Where this project sits

The contribution is not new theory. It is a controlled, systematically verified numerical isolation of the beta-effect as a single independent variable, spanning the regime from linear wave propagation through to nonlinear shear instability, in one consistent model and one consistent numerical framework, with quantified numerical uncertainty and an explicit link to observed atmospheric scales.

Three things distinguish it from a routine simulation study. First, verification and validation are treated as separate, explicitly reported activities rather than being conflated. Second, the rotation rate is treated as a free parameter and swept well beyond its terrestrial value, which turns a fixed physical constant into a genuine independent variable and allows the scaling of phase speed with Omega to be tested directly rather than assumed. Third, an idealised model result is closed back against reanalysis data of the real atmosphere, so the comparison is against observation and not only against theory.

### 2.4 Why the shallow-water model

The rotating shallow-water system is the minimal model that retains every ingredient the physics requires. It carries rotation, a free surface that supplies a finite deformation radius, full nonlinearity, and, when posed on a sphere rather than a tangent plane, the geometry from which beta emerges naturally rather than by imposed approximation. A three-dimensional primitive-equation model would add stratification and baroclinic processes that are not part of the research question and would obscure the single mechanism under study. A beta-plane model would insert the effect by hand rather than deriving it from geometry.

### 2.5 Why a spectral method on the sphere

Spherical harmonics are the eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on the sphere. They are therefore not an arbitrary numerical basis but the natural normal modes of the domain, and the analytic solutions being tested against, the Rossby-Haurwitz waves, are themselves single spherical harmonics. The basis is uniformly resolving, carries no polar coordinate singularity in the discretisation, and converges spectrally, meaning error falls faster than any fixed power of resolution for smooth solutions. This gives an unusually clean convergence study, which in turn supports rigorous uncertainty quantification.

## 4. Theoretical framework

### 4.1 Governing equations

The rotating shallow-water system on a sphere of radius R, in vector-invariant form, with horizontal velocity **u** tangent to the sphere and fluid depth h:

```
∂u/∂t + (ζ + f) k × u  =  -∇( g h + ½ |u|² )

∂h/∂t + ∇·( h u )  =  0
```

where

- `ζ = k · (∇ × u)` is the relative vorticity,
- `k` is the local outward unit normal to the sphere,
- `f = 2 Ω sin(φ)` is the Coriolis parameter at latitude φ,
- `g` is gravitational acceleration,
- `Ω` is the planetary rotation rate.

The vector-invariant form is chosen deliberately. It exposes the absolute vorticity `ζ + f` as the quantity multiplying the rotational term, which makes the beta-effect structurally visible in the equation rather than buried in a coordinate expansion, and it discretises cleanly in a spectral basis.

### 4.2 Geometry on the sphere

All differential operators are those of the curved manifold, not of a plane. The sphere carries the induced metric

```
ds² = R² ( dθ² + sin²θ dφ² )
```

with θ colatitude and φ longitude. Gradients, divergences and curls are defined through the Levi-Civita connection of this metric, and the covariant derivative introduces Christoffel-symbol terms absent in Cartesian geometry. Two consequences matter physically.

First, the metric terms are what make the advection operator on the sphere differ from its planar counterpart, and they are the reason a purely zonal flow experiences a curvature effect.

Second, and more importantly for this project, beta is a geometric quantity. Writing the meridional coordinate as `y = R φ_lat`, the gradient of the Coriolis parameter is

```
β = df/dy = (2 Ω / R) cos(φ)
```

so beta is set entirely by the rotation rate and the radius of curvature of the domain. It is not an assumption added to the model. It is what the geometry produces.

Vector fields are represented in spin-weighted spherical harmonics, which is the representation that handles the tangent-bundle structure of the sphere without coordinate singularity at the poles.

*Full derivation, including the metric, Christoffel symbols, covariant derivative and the reduction of the operators to spherical coordinates, belongs in the derivation appendix rather than the main text.*

### 4.3 Conserved quantities

The inviscid system conserves, as domain integrals over the sphere:

| Quantity | Expression | Role |
|----------|------------|------|
| Mass | ∫ h dA | Primary correctness diagnostic |
| Total energy | ∫ ( ½ h \|u\|² + ½ g h² ) dA | Kinetic plus available potential |
| Potential enstrophy | ∫ ½ h q² dA | Most sensitive diagnostic, degrades first |

Additionally, potential vorticity

```
q = ( ζ + f ) / h
```

is materially conserved following the flow,

```
Dq/Dt = 0
```

This is the single most important statement in the theory. The entire beta mechanism is a corollary of it. A column displaced poleward moves to larger f, and since q is conserved, ζ must decrease to compensate, generating the anticyclonic-cyclonic vorticity couplet whose induced flow propagates the pattern westward.

Numerically, the drift of these three integrals over a run is a direct measure of accumulated discretisation and dissipation error, and potential enstrophy is the strictest of the three.

### 4.4 Nondimensional parameters

| Parameter | Definition | Physical meaning | Target regime |
|-----------|------------|------------------|---------------|
| Rossby number | `Ro = U / (2 Ω L)` | Inertia relative to rotation | Ro ≪ 1, rotation-dominated |
| Deformation radius | `L_d = √(g H) / f` | Scale at which rotation balances gravity | Compared to wave scale |
| Burger number | `Bu = (L_d / L)²` | Stratification relative to rotation | Order unity or below |
| Nondimensional amplitude | `a = A / H` | Departure from linearity | a ≪ 1 for linear runs |

Nondimensionalisation with length scale L, velocity scale U and time scale L/U reduces the parameter space to these groups, which is what allows results obtained at non-terrestrial rotation rates to be interpreted as physics rather than as arbitrary numbers.

### 4.5 Linearisation I: the dispersion relation

Linearising about a state of rest with uniform depth H, and working in the local tangent-plane limit for interpretability, gives the barotropic Rossby-wave dispersion relation

```
ω = - β k / ( k² + l² + 1 / L_d² )
```

with zonal and meridional wavenumbers k and l. The westward zonal phase speed follows as

```
c = ω / k = - β / ( k² + l² + 1 / L_d² )
```

Three predictions follow immediately and are directly testable.

1. **The sign is always negative.** Phase propagation is westward for every mode. There is no eastward-propagating branch.
2. **Longer waves are faster.** Phase speed magnitude decreases monotonically with total wavenumber.
3. **Phase speed is proportional to beta**, and therefore proportional to Omega at fixed geometry.

### 4.6 The Rossby-Haurwitz solution on the sphere

On the sphere the correct global analogue replaces plane waves with spherical harmonics. For the nondivergent barotropic vorticity equation, a streamfunction consisting of a single spherical harmonic of degree n and order m is an exact solution that rotates rigidly westward with angular phase speed

```
c_angular = - 2 Ω / [ n ( n + 1 ) ]
```

This is the primary analytic target of the project. It is exact, it is parameter-free once n and Omega are fixed, and it predicts both scalings under test simultaneously. Phase speed should fall as `1 / [n(n+1)]` with mode degree and rise linearly with Omega.

The divergent shallow-water system used here is not exactly the nondivergent system, so a small, quantifiable departure is expected at finite deformation radius. Characterising that departure is itself a result, not an error.

### 4.7 Linearisation II: barotropic instability

Linearising instead about a zonal base state `ū(φ)` gives an eigenvalue problem for perturbation growth. The necessary condition for instability, the Rayleigh-Kuo criterion, is that the meridional gradient of absolute vorticity

```
dQ/dy = β - d²ū/dy²
```

changes sign somewhere in the domain. Equivalently, there must exist a latitude at which

```
β = d²ū/dy²
```

The physical reading is direct. Beta is stabilising and acts everywhere. Instability requires the curvature of the jet to be strong enough, somewhere, to overcome the planetary vorticity gradient. Since beta scales with Omega, faster rotation should stabilise a jet of fixed shape, which is a clean, testable prediction and the natural bridge between the two halves of the project.

The condition is necessary but not sufficient, so satisfying it does not guarantee growth. This asymmetry must be stated carefully when interpreting results.

### 4.8 Spectral representation

Scalar fields are expanded as

```
h(θ, φ, t) = Σ_{n,m} h_{n,m}(t) Y_n^m(θ, φ)
```

and vector fields in the corresponding spin-weighted harmonic basis. Because the Laplacian is diagonal in this basis, with eigenvalue `-n(n+1)/R²`, linear operators become trivially invertible and the analytic mode structure of the problem maps one-to-one onto the numerical representation.

## 5. Numerical method

### 5.1 Framework

Solver framework is Dedalus version 3, a spectral PDE framework with native support for the two-sphere. Language is Python. Fields are represented in a spherical-harmonic basis with spin-weighted harmonics for vector quantities.

### 5.2 Discretisation

- **Spatial.** Spectral Galerkin in `(Nφ, Nθ)` spherical-harmonic modes.
- **Dealiasing.** Three-halves rule, to eliminate aliasing error from quadratic nonlinear terms.
- **Temporal.** Implicit-explicit Runge-Kutta. Linear terms, including the fast gravity-wave terms that set the stiff timescale, are treated implicitly. Nonlinear advective terms are treated explicitly. This removes the gravity-wave restriction on the timestep while keeping the nonlinear treatment simple.
- **Dissipation.** Hyperdiffusion of the form `ν ∇⁴` or higher, with the coefficient scaled so that damping is negligible at the resolved scales of interest and acts only near the truncation wavenumber. The coefficient is a controlled variable and its influence is measured explicitly in the sensitivity study, not assumed negligible.

### 5.3 Baseline physical constants

Standard benchmark values, adopted unchanged so that results are directly comparable with published work.

| Constant | Symbol | Value | Units |
|----------|--------|-------|-------|
| Sphere radius | R | 6.37122 × 10⁶ | m |
| Rotation rate | Ω₀ | 7.292 × 10⁻⁵ | s⁻¹ |
| Gravitational acceleration | g | 9.80616 | m s⁻² |
| Mean fluid depth | H | 1 × 10⁴ | m |

### 5.5 Diagnostics written at runtime

1. Full `u` and `h` snapshots at fixed cadence, HDF5.
2. Derived relative vorticity and potential vorticity fields.
3. Conservation integrals for mass, energy and potential enstrophy, written every timestep.
4. Spherical-harmonic coefficient spectra at fixed cadence.
5. Longitude-time slices at selected latitudes for Hovmöller construction.
6. Scalar time series of domain-maximum velocity, for stability monitoring and growth-rate fitting.

### 5.6 Compute environment

GPU-backed or multi-core cloud compute, run under MPI where parallelism is beneficial. Environment fully specified in the reproducibility manifest, including framework version, dependency versions and random seeds for any stochastic initialisation.

### 6.4 A note on the rotation-rate sweep

Sweeping Omega is what converts a physical constant into an independent variable. It changes beta directly, since beta is proportional to Omega, while leaving the geometry untouched. Rotation rate also changes the deformation radius, so the sweep is not a pure beta sweep and this confound must be acknowledged and, where possible, controlled by tracking the Burger number across the sweep.

## 7. Data architecture

Three tiers. Tier 1 is generated by the project and constitutes the primary dataset. Tiers 2 and 3 are external and are used exclusively for verification and validation respectively.

### 7.1 Tier 1: self-generated simulation output (primary)

**Nature.** All primary data is produced by the project's own numerical experiments. Each run writes field snapshots and diagnostic time series.

**Output schema per run.**

| Product | Format | Contents | Cadence |
|---------|--------|----------|---------|
| Field snapshots | HDF5 | u (vector), h (scalar), ζ, q on the full sphere | Fixed simulation-time interval |
| Conservation series | HDF5 or CSV | Mass, energy, potential enstrophy integrals | Every timestep |
| Spectra | HDF5 | Spherical-harmonic coefficient amplitudes | Fixed interval |
| Hovmöller slices | HDF5 | Depth or vorticity along longitude at selected latitudes | High cadence |
| Run log | Text | Full parameter set, environment, wall time, stability warnings | Once per run |

**Procedures applied to Tier 1 data.**

1. Phase-speed extraction by linear fit to phase lines in longitude-time space, with an independent cross-check from the argument of the complex spectral coefficient of the excited mode.
2. Spectral decomposition into spherical-harmonic and zonal-Fourier components.
3. Conservation-drift quantification over the full integration.
4. Meridional structure extraction and turning-latitude identification.
5. Exponential growth-rate fitting over an identified linear window for instability runs.

**Volume estimate.** Dominated by snapshot cadence and resolution. Snapshot interval should be chosen to resolve the wave period by roughly twenty samples, not more, and full-field output should be reduced or disabled for parameter-sweep runs where only Hovmöller slices and scalar diagnostics are required.

### 7.2 Tier 2: benchmark verification data (external)

**Source.** The standard test set for numerical approximations to the shallow-water equations in spherical geometry (Williamson et al., 1992). This is the accepted benchmark suite for numerical methods in this domain and consists of seven cases spanning advection across the poles, steady geostrophically balanced flow, forced nonlinear advection, zonal flow over an isolated mountain, Rossby-Haurwitz waves and observed atmospheric states.

**Cases used.**

| Case | Description | Reference type | Purpose |
|------|-------------|----------------|---------|
| 1 | Cosine-bell advection across the poles | Closed-form | Tests advection and polar handling |
| 2 | Steady-state nonlinear geostrophic flow | Closed-form analytic | Primary accuracy test |
| 5 | Zonal flow over an isolated mountain | High-resolution numerical | Tests topographic forcing and nonlinearity |
| 6 | Rossby-Haurwitz wave | High-resolution numerical | Directly relevant to the physics under study |

**Handling of cases without closed-form solutions.** Cases 5 and 6 have no exact solution. The literature standard is to use high-resolution spectral-transform integrations as reference solutions against which lower-resolution and alternative schemes are evaluated. The original reference solutions were distributed via NCAR through legacy anonymous FTP and the associated technical notes, and the published solutions are documented in the NCAR technical note series.

**Adopted strategy, and it is deliberate.** The project does not depend on retrieving legacy reference files. Instead, reference solutions for cases 5 and 6 are generated in-house by running the project's own solver at the L3 resolution rung, and lower-resolution runs are evaluated against that. Published error magnitudes in the literature are used as an external sanity check on the resulting numbers. This converts an availability risk into a methodological strength and is standard practice.

**Procedures applied to Tier 2 data.** Computation of `l₂` and `l∞` error norms for depth and velocity fields at fixed evaluation times; spectral-convergence study relating error to truncation wavenumber; conservation-drift comparison.

### 7.3 Tier 3: observational reanalysis data (external)

**Primary dataset.** ERA5 hourly data on pressure levels, from the Copernicus Climate Data Store. ERA5 is the fifth-generation ECMWF atmospheric reanalysis, combining model fields with global observations by data assimilation into a physically consistent gridded record. It is provided on a regular latitude-longitude grid at 0.25 degree resolution on 37 pressure levels, available from 1940 to near-present.

**Request specification.**

| Field | Specification |
|-------|---------------|
| Dataset | ERA5 hourly data on pressure levels from 1940 to present |
| Variables | Geopotential; u-component of wind; v-component of wind; relative vorticity (optional) |
| Pressure levels | 250 hPa and 300 hPa (jet core); 500 hPa (mid-tropospheric wave structure) |
| Time window | One Northern Hemisphere winter season, December to February |
| Temporal sampling | Six-hourly or daily |
| Spatial grid | Regridded to 1.0 degree; full 0.25 degree resolution is unnecessary and inflates volume |
| Format | NetCDF |
| Access | Copernicus Climate Data Store, free registration, web form or the cdsapi Python client |

**Access notes.** Registration is required and the dataset licence must be accepted before the first request or it will fail. The CDS has been migrated to a new system, so the current cdsapi client and a freshly generated API key are required. Requests are queued and can take hours, so acquisition must not sit on the critical path.

**Fallback dataset.** NCEP/NCAR Reanalysis 1, distributed by NOAA PSL. Lower resolution, simpler direct NetCDF download, no queueing. Adequate for every diagnostic required here.

**Procedures applied to Tier 3 data.**

1. Compute the zonal-mean zonal wind profile `ū(φ)` at the jet-core level, seasonally averaged.
2. Differentiate twice in latitude to obtain `d²ū/dy²`, and evaluate `β - d²ū/dy²` to locate sign changes and test the Rayleigh-Kuo condition against a real profile.
3. Use the smoothed observed profile as the initial condition for the observationally-seeded instability run.
4. Fourier-decompose 500 hPa geopotential in longitude to extract dominant zonal wavenumbers.
5. Track phase of the dominant modes over time to estimate observed westward drift for scale comparison against model output.

**Scope discipline.** ERA5 is used for scale comparison and jet-profile extraction only. The project does not attempt to reproduce observed atmospheric evolution, and no such claim should be made. The real atmosphere is baroclinic and three-dimensional; the model is neither.

### 7.4 Tier 4: constants and parameter provenance

All physical constants are taken from the benchmark standard values (section 5.3) and cited to the benchmark suite rather than to any code implementation. This guarantees comparability with published results and provides a clean provenance chain for the controlled variables.

### 7.5 Optional cross-check

A public spherical shallow-water dataset generated by an independent spectral solver on an equiangular latitude-longitude grid with Earth-matched parameters is available through the torch-harmonics package. It may be used as an independent sanity check on solver behaviour. It is explicitly optional and is not a primary source.

## 9. Verification protocol

Verification answers one question only. **Is the code solving the intended equations correctly?** It says nothing about whether those equations describe reality. That is section 10, and the two must not be conflated in the write-up.

### 9.1 Error norms

For a computed field `X` against reference `X_ref`, over the sphere with area element dA:

```
l₂  = sqrt( ∫ (X - X_ref)² dA  /  ∫ X_ref² dA )

l∞  = max |X - X_ref|  /  max |X_ref|
```

Both are computed for depth and for each velocity component, at fixed evaluation times.

### 9.2 Convergence study

Error is computed across the full resolution ladder for benchmark case 2, which has an exact solution, and the observed convergence behaviour is compared against the spectral expectation. For smooth solutions in a spectral method, error should decay faster than any fixed algebraic power of resolution until the floor set by time-integration error or machine precision is reached. Identifying which floor is reached, and at what resolution, is a reportable result.

### 9.3 Conservation criteria

| Quantity | Tolerance over full integration | Interpretation if exceeded |
|----------|--------------------------------|----------------------------|
| Mass | Relative drift below 10⁻¹⁰ | Implementation error, investigate immediately |
| Energy | Relative drift small and decreasing with resolution | Excess hyperdiffusion or timestep too large |
| Potential enstrophy | Monotonic slow decay acceptable, growth is not | Growth indicates numerical instability |

Potential enstrophy is the strictest diagnostic and should be treated as the early-warning signal.

### 9.4 Pass criteria

The verification gate is passed when, jointly:

1. Case 2 error norms fall at the expected spectral rate across the ladder.
2. Case 2 and case 1 error magnitudes at production resolution are consistent in order of magnitude with published values for spectral methods.
3. Mass drift is at round-off level.
4. Potential enstrophy does not grow.
5. Case 6 phase behaviour at L1 matches the L3 reference within a stated tolerance.

Failure at any point halts the project until resolved. This gate is not optional and not negotiable, because every downstream result inherits its validity from it.

## 10. Validation protocol

Validation answers the different question. **Do the equations, correctly solved, describe the physics they are meant to describe?**

### 10.1 Against analytic linear theory

Measured phase speeds from the P campaign are compared against:

- the tangent-plane dispersion relation of section 4.5, for qualitative scaling;
- the exact Rossby-Haurwitz angular phase speed `-2Ω/[n(n+1)]` of section 4.6, for quantitative agreement.

Reported as a scatter of measured against predicted phase speed with a fitted slope, and as residuals plotted against mode degree and against rotation rate to expose any systematic structure.

### 10.2 Against the expected scalings

Two independent power-law tests:

1. `c_ang` against `1/[n(n+1)]` at fixed Omega. Expect a straight line through the origin.
2. `c_ang` against Omega at fixed n. Expect linear proportionality.

Deviations at high n are anticipated, since finite deformation radius and hyperdiffusion both act preferentially at small scales. Quantifying and explaining these deviations is a result, not a failure.

### 10.3 Against linear stability theory

For each instability run, the measured growth rate and dominant wavenumber are compared against the Rayleigh-Kuo diagnostic computed from the base-state profile. The specific test is whether jets that fail the necessary condition remain stable, and whether jets that satisfy it grow. Because the condition is necessary and not sufficient, a stable run that satisfies the criterion is consistent with theory and must be reported as such rather than as a discrepancy.

### 10.4 Against observation

Model wavelengths, zonal wavenumbers and westward drift speeds are compared, at the level of order of magnitude and dominant wavenumber, against the reanalysis diagnostics of section 7.3. The comparison is explicitly one of scales, not of trajectories. The stated expectation is agreement in dominant zonal wavenumber and in the order of magnitude of drift speed, with disagreement in detail attributable to baroclinicity, vertical structure and forcing absent from the model.

## 11. Uncertainty quantification

Every reported number carries an uncertainty derived from one of the following sources.

### 11.1 Discretisation error

Estimated by Richardson-type extrapolation across the resolution ladder. For a quantity `Q` computed at successively refined resolutions, the difference between successive rungs bounds the discretisation error at the coarser one, and the extrapolated limit estimates the resolution-independent value. Reported as an error bar on every phase speed and growth rate quoted at production resolution.

### 11.2 Temporal truncation

Assessed by repeating a representative run at halved timestep and reporting the change in measured quantities.

### 11.3 Hyperdiffusion sensitivity

The dissipation coefficient is not physical and its value is a modelling choice. Runs P-18 and equivalents vary it across a bracketing range and report the induced spread in phase speed. If that spread is comparable to the effect under study at any mode degree, results at that degree must be reported as dissipation-limited and excluded from quantitative scaling fits.

### 11.4 Fitting uncertainty

Phase speeds are obtained by linear fits to phase lines, and growth rates by linear fits to log-energy over a selected window. Both carry standard fit uncertainty, and growth rates additionally carry sensitivity to the choice of fitting window. The window-selection sensitivity is quantified by refitting over shifted windows and reporting the spread.

### 11.5 Seed sensitivity

Instability runs initialised from random perturbations are repeated across seeds (I-12), and the spread in measured growth rate across seeds is reported as an uncertainty on the growth rate.

### 11.6 Reporting convention

No headline number is reported without an accompanying uncertainty, and the dominant contributing source is named in each case.

## 13. Expected results and falsifiable predictions

Stated in advance, so that agreement is a test rather than a retrofit.

### 13.1 Predictions

| ID | Prediction | Falsified by |
|----|------------|--------------|
| H1 | All initialised modes propagate westward, without exception | Any resolved eastward-propagating barotropic mode |
| H2 | Angular phase speed is proportional to `1/[n(n+1)]` at fixed Ω | Systematic departure from linearity beyond stated uncertainty at well-resolved n |
| H3 | Angular phase speed is proportional to Ω at fixed n | Nonlinear or saturating dependence on Ω |
| H4 | Measured phase speeds agree with `-2Ω/[n(n+1)]` to within a few per cent at low n | Agreement worse than the quantified numerical uncertainty |
| H5 | Departure from the nondivergent prediction grows as the wave scale approaches the deformation radius | No systematic dependence on Burger number |
| H6 | Benchmark error decays spectrally with resolution | Algebraic convergence, indicating an implementation error |
| H7 | Jets failing the Rayleigh-Kuo condition remain stable | Growth in a run whose base state has no sign change in `dQ/dy` |
| H8 | Growth rate increases with supercriticality above the threshold | No systematic relation between shear and growth rate |
| H9 | Increasing Ω at fixed jet shape stabilises the flow | Growth rate independent of, or increasing with, Ω |
| H10 | Model dominant zonal wavenumber is of the same order as the observed dominant wavenumber | Order-of-magnitude disagreement in wavenumber |

### 13.2 On negative results

H7 is the strongest test in the set, because it is a genuine prohibition rather than a fitted agreement. A violation would indicate either an implementation error or a misapplication of the criterion, and either finding is reportable.

H5 is the prediction most likely to produce an interesting result, since it probes exactly where the divergent shallow-water system departs from the nondivergent theory that supplies the analytic target.

A result in which measured phase speeds agree with theory only after excluding poorly resolved modes is a legitimate and expected outcome, provided the exclusion criterion is stated in advance and applied uniformly.

### 17.2 Glossary

**Barotropic.** Flow with no vertical variation in structure, so density surfaces coincide with pressure surfaces. The single-layer shallow-water system is barotropic by construction.

**Beta-effect.** The dynamical consequence of the latitudinal variation of the Coriolis parameter on a rotating sphere.

**Deformation radius.** The horizontal length scale at which rotational and gravitational effects are comparable. Motions much larger than it are rotation-dominated.

**Hovmöller diagram.** A plot of a field against longitude on one axis and time on the other, in which a propagating wave appears as a tilted line whose slope is its phase speed.

**Hyperdiffusion.** A dissipation term using a high-order derivative operator, chosen so that damping acts almost exclusively at the smallest resolved scales and leaves large scales nearly untouched.

**IMEX.** Implicit-explicit time integration. Stiff linear terms are advanced implicitly for stability while nonlinear terms are advanced explicitly for simplicity.

**Potential enstrophy.** The domain integral of half the depth-weighted squared potential vorticity. An inviscid invariant and the most sensitive numerical health diagnostic available.

**Potential vorticity.** Absolute vorticity divided by fluid depth. Materially conserved in the inviscid system, and the quantity from which the entire beta mechanism follows.

**Rayleigh-Kuo criterion.** A necessary condition for barotropic instability, requiring that the meridional gradient of absolute vorticity change sign somewhere in the domain.

**Reanalysis.** A physically consistent gridded reconstruction of the past atmosphere, produced by assimilating historical observations into a fixed numerical weather-prediction model.

**Rossby-Haurwitz wave.** An exact solution of the nondivergent barotropic vorticity equation on a sphere consisting of a single spherical harmonic, which rotates rigidly westward.

**Spectral Galerkin method.** A numerical method that represents the solution as a truncated expansion in globally smooth basis functions and enforces the equations by orthogonality against those functions.

**Spin-weighted spherical harmonics.** The generalisation of spherical harmonics appropriate to vector and tensor fields on the sphere, which avoids coordinate singularities at the poles.

**Turning latitude.** The latitude beyond which a wave becomes evanescent rather than propagating, so its amplitude decays.

**Validation.** Determining whether the chosen equations describe the physical system of interest.

**Verification.** Determining whether the code solves the chosen equations correctly. Distinct from validation, and always performed first.
